## Act 1: The Hook (The System Crisis)

You just deployed an update to an ATM application system exactly at midnight. Suddenly, the system just stubbornly stops processing.

CPU usage is at 1%, Memory is normal, no Error Logs, no Crash Reports. The system is simply "frozen".

When you inspect the currently running processes, you find that there are 2 money transfer threads running at the exact same millisecond:
- Thread A is trying to transfer money from Account 1 to Account 2
- Thread B is trying to transfer money from Account 2 to Account 1

And everything just hangs there... What happened?

## Act 2: The Mental Model

To visualize what happens in the CPU, imagine a 4-way intersection without traffic lights.

4 cars arrive at the intersection at the exact same time. Traffic laws state: "You must always yield to the car on your right."

- Car 1 stops for Car 2
- Car 2 stops for Car 3
- Car 3 stops for Car 4
- Car 4 stops for Car 1!

No one can move. The intersection is 100% safe (no crashes), but everything is at a standstill. The resource (the middle of the intersection) is reserved but no one can actually use it.

## Act 3: The Rabbit Hole

This circular traffic jam situation is what Computer Science calls a **Deadlock**.

For a Deadlock to occur, 4 conditions must happen simultaneously (known as Coffman Conditions):
1. **Mutual Exclusion:** Resources can only be used by one entity at a time.
2. **Hold and Wait:** Holding on to current resources while waiting for new ones.
3. **No Preemption:** Resources cannot be forcefully taken away.
4. **Circular Wait:** Waiting forms a circular loop.

Solving and preventing your banking app from freezing requires writing code to break at least one of these conditions.
