## Question 3: Difference between 2 sampling methods (Stratified vs. Simple Random)

### Problem

Want to sample $n$ from a population with $k$ subgroups.

- **Method 1:** Stratified Sampling (proportional selection)
- **Method 2:** Simple Random Sampling (random pool, ignoring groups)

### Difference

The core lies in **"probability of getting representatives from all groups"** and **"Precision"**.

#### 1. Representation Guarantee

**Method 1 (Stratified):**
- 100% guarantee to get data from all $k$ groups according to their real proportions.
- This method is excellent if our data is **Imbalanced** (e.g. contains very rare groups).

<Callout title="📌 Example: University Opinion Poll" type="info">
There are 9,000 students and 100 professors.

If sampling via **Stratified**, we force a quota that professors must be included.
</Callout>

**Method 2 (Simple Random):**
- Relies on luck. If any group $k$ is very small, there's a high chance we **"won't sample them at all"**.
- Causing that group's data to be missing (Under-represented).

<Callout title="Example: Simple Random Problem" type="warn">
From the university case, if randomly pooled, we might only get students and no professors, causing a skewed poll result.
</Callout>

#### 2. Standard Error

| Sampling Method | Error Value | Reason |
|------

---|----------|--------|
| **Stratified** | Lower | Guarantees population structure. The obtained data is better distributed. |
| **Simple Random** | Higher | Especially if each group is highly diverse. |

### Conclusion

<Callout title="✅ Recommendation" type="success">
If you know the data is already divided into groups and want each group to be represented, you must use **Stratified**.

(This is also the Standard for splitting Train/Test sets in Classification tasks).
</Callout>

## Question 4: Entropy Calculation (Uncertainty value)

### Data

Total 8 balls:
- 🔴 Red: 4 balls
- 🟢 Green: 2 balls
- 🔵 Blue: 2 balls

### Entropy Formula:

$$H(X) = - \sum_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i) \log_2 p(x_i)$$

### Calculation Steps

#### 1. Find the probability ($p$) of each color

$$
P(\text{Red}) = \frac{4}{8} = 0.5
$$
$$
P(\text{Green}) = \frac{2}{8} = 0.25
$$
$$
P(\text{Blue}) = \frac{2}{8} = 0.25
$$

#### 2. Substitute values into the formula

We must calculate term by term and sum them up (don't forget the minus sign at the front):

$$
H(X) = - [ (0.5 \log_2 0.5) + (0.25 \log_2 0.25) + (0.25 \log_2 0.25) ]
$$

#### 3. Convert Log values (base 2):
$$
\log_2(0.5) = \log_2(2^{-1}) = -1
$$
$$
\log_2(0.25) = \log_2(2^{-2}) = -2
$$

#### 4. Calculate numbers

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Term Red} &= 0.5 \times (-1) = -0.5 \\
\text{Term Green} &= 0.25 \times (-2) = -0.5 \\
\text{Term Blue} &= 0.25 \times (-2) = -0.5
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sum inside bracket} &= -0.5 + (-0.5) + (-0.5) = -1.5 \\
H(X) &= - (-1.5) \\
H(X) &= \mathbf{1.5} \text{ bits}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Answer

<Callout title="✅ Result" type="success">
The Entropy value is **1.5 bits**.
</Callout>

## Data Mining Connection Trivia

### What is the maximum possible value (Max Entropy) for this problem?

If 3 ball colors have exactly equal amounts (equal occurrence probability), the highest Entropy value would be $\log_2(3) \approx 1.585$ bits.

<Callout title="💡 Result Interpretation" type="info">
Our calculated value of **1.5**, which is almost the Max, means this dataset **"has high uncertainty"** (hard to guess what color will be drawn, even though Red is the most abundant).
</Callout>

### Connection with Decision Tree

**In Decision Tree:**
- We like Nodes with **low Entropy** (e.g. all Red = Entropy 0).
- Because it means the data is **Pure**.
- The purer it is, the more accurate the prediction!
